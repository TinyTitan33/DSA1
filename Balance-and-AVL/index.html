Why height matters: search comparisons

Searching follows one path from the root down toward the target, making one comparison per visited node. A tree of height 3 can have a root-to-leaf path of up to 4 nodes (4 comparisons), while a height-14 chain has 15 nodes (up to 15 comparisons).

Balanced BST / AVL: O(log n) — height 3 holds 15 items with at most 4 comparisons.
Worst-case unbalanced BST: O(n) — sorted chain has height 14 and up to 15 comparisons.

Rotation rules (4 cases)

Sign convention: − means left is taller, + means right is taller. Look at the broken node (−− or ++) and its taller child.

Right (single)

−− and left child −, so single rotation (right).

Left (single)

++ and right child +, so single rotation (left).

Left-Right (double)

−− and left child +, so double rotation (left-right).

Right-Left (double)

++ and right child −, so double rotation (right-left).
